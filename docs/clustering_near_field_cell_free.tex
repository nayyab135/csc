\begin{keybox}{Summary in five lines}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item In your code, the channel-norm (CN) and information-rate (IR) clusters are almost identical by construction, so their BER and sum-rate curves overlap (Section~\ref{sec:audit}).
  \item BER and sum-rate rarely separate clustering rules in your system, because the cross-AP detector re-reads the non-serving APs whenever the cluster estimate is unreliable \cite{haider2026icassp,haider2026vtc}. A worse cluster costs \emph{fronthaul}, not BER.
  \item Metrics that \emph{do} separate rules: fronthaul, gate firing rate, captured information, energy efficiency, tail SE and fairness, and above all the fronthaul needed to reach a target BER (Section~\ref{sec:metrics}).
  \item Proposed: a \textbf{detector-aware information-surplus clustering} with a closed-form fronthaul-optimal cluster size, with a bound that links the cluster to $\Delta_k$ of your papers (Section~\ref{sec:proposals}).
  \item Show the gain in an \textbf{interference-limited} regime, at \textbf{equal fronthaul} or \textbf{equal BER} (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{keybox}

\section{What your code does today (audit)}
\label{sec:audit}

\subsection{Channel-norm (CN) cluster}
For each user $k$ the code uses a metric and a threshold per propagation regime (code inspection, done here):
\begin{equation}
  m_{l,k}=\begin{cases}\|\mathbf h^{\rm NF}_{l,k}\|^2\approx N\beta_{l,k}, & A_{l,k}=1,\\ \beta_{l,k}, & A_{l,k}=0,\end{cases}\qquad
  D^{\rm CN}_{l,k}=\mathbb 1\Big[m_{l,k}\ge \tfrac{1}{c}\max_{l'\in\mathcal K^{\rm reg}_k} m_{l',k}\Big],
\end{equation}
with $c=$\,\texttt{clu\_div}$=2$, plus the master AP $\arg\max_l m_{l,k}$. A threshold on channel gain with a master AP follows the dynamic cooperation clustering (DCC) idea of \cite{bjornson2020scalable}.

\textbf{Issue 1 (inconsistent scale).} A near-field metric is about $N\beta$ and a far-field metric is $\beta$, so the master AP is almost always a near-field AP even when a far-field AP has a larger $\beta$. Use the same scale for both regimes.

\subsection{Information-rate (IR / ``BSR'') cluster}
\textbf{What the paper does.} Mashdour and de~Lamare define, for AP $m$ and user $k$ (downlink model with precoder $\mathbf p_k$, estimate $\hat g_{km}$ and error $\tilde g_{km}$) \cite[eq.~(11)]{mashdour2024study}
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{SR}_{km}=\log_2\!\Big(1+\frac{\rho_f|\hat g_{km}|^2\mathbf p_k^{\mathsf H}\mathbf p_k}{\rho_f|\tilde g_{km}|^2\mathbf p_k^{\mathsf H}\mathbf p_k+\sigma_w^2}\Big),
  \label{eq:bsr}
\end{equation}
a network-wide threshold equal to the \emph{average} rate \cite[eq.~(12)]{mashdour2024study}
\begin{equation}
  s_{rc}=\frac1{KM}\sum_{m=1}^M\sum_{k=1}^K\mathrm{SR}_{km},
\end{equation}
and the cluster \cite[eq.~(13), Algorithm~1]{mashdour2024study}
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal U_k=\{m:\mathrm{SR}_{km}\ge s_{rc}\}\cup\arg\max_m \mathrm{SR}_{km}.
\end{equation}
The rate in \eqref{eq:bsr} has \emph{no inter-user interference term}; it depends on the estimated gain, the estimation error and the noise, and therefore includes small-scale fading \cite[Sec.~III-B]{mashdour2024study}.

\textbf{What your code does.} It scores each link by
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{SR}_{l,k}=\log_2\!\Big(1+\frac{p\,(\mathrm{tr}\,\mathbf R_{l,k}-\mathrm{tr}\,\mathbf C_{l,k})}{p\,\mathrm{tr}\,\mathbf C_{l,k}+1}\Big),
\end{equation}
which has the same structure as \eqref{eq:bsr} (signal over estimation error plus noise), but in statistical form. It then \emph{ranks} APs by this score and keeps \emph{exactly as many APs as the CN cluster has}.

\textbf{Issue 2 (size rule differs from the paper).} The paper's cluster size comes from the average-rate threshold \cite[eqs.~(12)--(13)]{mashdour2024study}; the code copies $|\mathcal S^{\rm CN}_k|$ instead. With orthogonal pilots and good estimates, $\mathrm{tr}\,\mathbf C\approx0$, so the score is monotone in $\mathrm{tr}\,\mathbf R_{l,k}\approx N\beta_{l,k}$ and ranks APs exactly like CN. With the same size and the same ranking, the clusters coincide; the code's console line ``AP-selection disagreement: CN vs Rate'' reports this.

\textbf{Issue 3 (shared limitation).} Neither \eqref{eq:bsr} nor the code score accounts for interference from other users, or for what the detector does with the cluster. This is the opening for the proposals in Section~\ref{sec:proposals}.

\begin{keybox}{Consequence}
To compare fairly with \cite{mashdour2024study}, implement its average-rate threshold (eqs.~(12)--(13)) instead of the CN size. Any new rule must choose its own size and use information that CN does not use (interference or detector behaviour); otherwise it will again coincide with CN.
\end{keybox}

\subsection{Why BER and sum-rate hardly move}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The detector compensates.} When the reliability test fires, cross-AP list SIC fuses all APs \cite[Sec.~III-B]{haider2026vtc}, at an extra per-user fronthaul cost $\bar B_k=|\mathcal S_k|+\rho(L-|\mathcal S_k|)$ \cite[Sec.~III-B]{haider2026vtc}. A weaker cluster makes the gate fire more often: the cost appears as fronthaul, not as BER.
  \item \textbf{Noise-limited regime.} With $K=8$, $N=64$ and $L=8$, the local MMSE combiners suppress most interference; an earlier GBSE test in this project gave clusters of about 1.3 APs and no visible gain.
  \item \textbf{Shared combiner.} SIC, list-SIC and cross-AP share the same combiner, so the combiner-SINR sum rate cannot separate them.
\end{itemize}
The GBSA study reaches a related conclusion from the energy side: replacing its energy-efficiency objective by sum-rate or channel-norm objectives gives clearly lower energy efficiency at the same cluster-size limit \cite[Sec.~VI, Fig.~2]{tesolin2026gbsa}. The choice of \emph{metric} decides what a clustering rule appears to achieve.

\section{Metrics that prove a clustering is better}
\label{sec:metrics}

$\mathcal S_k$ is the cluster of user $k$, $\gamma_{l,k}$ the per-AP post-combining SINR (eq.~(23) of \cite{haider2026vtc}), $\Gamma_k=\sum_{l=1}^L\gamma_{l,k}$ and $\Gamma_{\mathcal S_k}=\sum_{l\in\mathcal S_k}\gamma_{l,k}$.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{M\arabic*.}]
  \item \textbf{Fronthaul per user} (from \cite[Sec.~III-B]{haider2026vtc}):
  \begin{equation}
    \bar B=\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^K\big(|\mathcal S_k|+\rho_k\,(L-|\mathcal S_k|)\big),
  \end{equation}
  with $\rho_k$ the measured gate firing rate (\texttt{etaXap\_acc} in your code). Fronthaul is the primary metric in \cite{lai2024hybridua}.
  \item \textbf{Iso-performance cost} (proposed here): the fronthaul $\bar B$ or energy needed to reach a target BER, e.g.\ $10^{-3}$.
  \item \textbf{Cluster size} $|\mathcal S_k|$: mean and histogram, split into near- and far-field-dominated users. Average cluster size is also reported in \cite[Fig.~3]{tesolin2026gbsa}.
  \item \textbf{Captured-information ratio} (proposed here) $\kappa_k=\Gamma_{\mathcal S_k}/\Gamma_k$, and the information surplus $\Delta_k$ (eq.~(26) of \cite{haider2026vtc}). Plot their CDFs.
  \item \textbf{Gate firing rate} $\rho$ vs.\ SNR (already measured by your code).
  \item \textbf{Tail performance}: 5th-percentile per-user SE (5\% outage), as in \cite{lai2024hybridua}.
  \item \textbf{Fairness}: Jain's index \cite{jain1984fairness}, also used in \cite{lai2024hybridua},
  \begin{equation}
    J=\frac{\big(\sum_k \mathrm{SE}_k\big)^2}{K\sum_k \mathrm{SE}_k^2}\in\Big[\tfrac1K,1\Big].
  \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Energy efficiency}. GBSA defines RANEE as the sum rate over $L P_{\rm circuit}+\sum_l p_l$, where $P_{\rm circuit}$ covers baseband, fronthaul, synchronisation and cooling \cite[eq.~(5)]{tesolin2026gbsa}. To make the fronthaul effect of your detector visible, split out a per-scalar fronthaul term (proposed here):
  \begin{equation}
    \mathrm{EE}=\frac{B_w\sum_k \mathrm{SE}_k}{L\,P_{\rm circ}+\sum_l p_l+P_{\rm FH}\,K\bar B}.
  \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Complexity}: AP-side combiner cost, CPU-side LSFD cost $\sum_k\mathcal O(|\mathcal S_k|^3)$, and the run time of the clustering algorithm, as in \cite[Fig.~1]{tesolin2026gbse} and \cite[Fig.~3]{tesolin2026gbsa}.
  \item \textbf{Load balance}: $\max_l|\mathcal D_l|/\big(\tfrac1L\sum_l|\mathcal D_l|\big)$; users per CPU is reported in \cite[Sec.~IV]{lai2024hybridua}.
  \item \textbf{Robustness to CSI error}: ratio of a metric under estimated vs.\ perfect CSI, per clustering rule.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{keybox}{Which ones to put in the paper}
M1, M2, M4 (CDF of $\kappa_k$ or $\Delta_k$), M6 and M8. Make M2 the headline figure: BER vs.\ fronthaul at fixed SNR, one curve per clustering rule.
\end{keybox}

\section{Proposed clustering strategies}
\label{sec:proposals}

\subsection{P1: Detector-aware information-surplus clustering (main proposal, proposed here)}
Choose, for each user, the \emph{smallest} set of APs that captures a fraction $1-\varepsilon$ of the user's total post-combining SINR, computed from the estimates of all AP--UE pairs (available after the cross-AP estimation of \cite{haider2026vtc}) and including interference:
\begin{equation}
  \hat\gamma_{l,k}=\frac{p\,|\hat{\mathbf v}_{l,k}^{\mathsf H}\hat{\mathbf h}_{l,k}|^2}
  {p\sum_{j\ne k}|\hat{\mathbf v}_{l,k}^{\mathsf H}\hat{\mathbf h}_{l,j}|^2+\hat{\mathbf v}_{l,k}^{\mathsf H}\big(p\sum_j\mathbf C_{l,j}+\mathbf I\big)\hat{\mathbf v}_{l,k}},
  \qquad
  \mathcal S_k=\arg\min_{\mathcal S}\Big\{|\mathcal S|:\ \sum_{l\in\mathcal S}\hat\gamma_{l,k}\ge(1-\varepsilon)\sum_{l=1}^L\hat\gamma_{l,k}\Big\}.
  \label{eq:p1}
\end{equation}
The SINR form follows eqs.~(23)--(24) of \cite{haider2026vtc} with the estimation-error covariance added; the minimum is found by sorting $\hat\gamma_{l,k}$ ($\mathcal O(L\log L)$ per user). Unlike \eqref{eq:bsr}, the score contains the interference left by the combiner.

\begin{proposition}[Cluster target bounds the information surplus; derived here]
If $\kappa_k=\Gamma_{\mathcal S_k}/\Gamma_k\ge 1-\varepsilon$, then the surplus of eq.~(26) of \cite{haider2026vtc} satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_k=\frac{\Gamma_k-\Gamma_{\mathcal S_k}}{(1+\Gamma_{\mathcal S_k})(1+\Gamma_k)}\;\le\;\frac{\varepsilon}{1+(1-\varepsilon)\Gamma_k}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\Gamma_k-\Gamma_{\mathcal S_k}\le\varepsilon\Gamma_k$ and $1+\Gamma_{\mathcal S_k}\ge1+(1-\varepsilon)\Gamma_k$, so $\Delta_k\le \varepsilon\Gamma_k/\big((1+(1-\varepsilon)\Gamma_k)(1+\Gamma_k)\big)$, and $\Gamma_k/(1+\Gamma_k)\le1$.
\end{proof}
One parameter $\varepsilon$ controls how much the cluster-only estimate can lose against the all-AP estimate, which links clustering to the detector analysis of \cite{haider2026icassp,haider2026vtc}. The cluster size adapts per user, which is where fronthaul savings come from.

\subsection{P2: Fronthaul-optimal cluster size for the gated cross-AP detector (proposed here)}
The cluster scalars are always forwarded, and the other $L-|\mathcal S_k|$ only when the gate fires \cite[Sec.~III-B]{haider2026vtc}. A larger cluster costs more base fronthaul but makes the gate fire less.

\textbf{Gate model (derived here).} The code declares a decision unreliable when the soft estimate is farther than $d_{\rm abs}=d_{\rm th}\,d_{\min}/2$ from every constellation point (the shadow-region test, eq.~(20) of \cite{haider2026vtc}). If the fused error is $\mathcal{CN}(0,\varepsilon^2_{\mathcal S})$ with $\varepsilon^2_{\mathcal S}=1/(1+\Gamma_{\mathcal S})$ (eq.~(25) of \cite{haider2026vtc}), the probability of staying inside the disk around the true point is $1-e^{-d_{\rm abs}^2/\varepsilon_{\mathcal S}^2}$, so
\begin{equation}
  \rho_k(\mathcal S)\approx\exp\!\big(-d_{\rm abs}^2\,(1+\Gamma_{\mathcal S})\big).
\end{equation}
\textbf{Optimal size.} With APs sorted by $\hat\gamma_{l,k}$ and $\mathcal S^{(m)}$ the $m$ best,
\begin{equation}
  m_k^\star=\arg\min_{m\in\{1,\dots,L\}}\Big[m+\exp\!\big(-d_{\rm abs}^2(1+\Gamma_{\mathcal S^{(m)}})\big)(L-m)\Big],
  \label{eq:p2}
\end{equation}
a search over $L$ values per user. P2 picks the size, P1's ranking picks the APs. The model can be validated against the measured \texttt{etaXap\_acc}.

\subsection{P3: Near-field separability-aware ranking (proposed here)}
In the near field an AP is valuable for user $k$ if it can separate $k$ from its strongest interferers in angle \emph{or range}. Polar-domain estimation gives estimated angles and ranges for every pair \cite{cui2022channel}, so the CPU can predict
\begin{equation}
  \hat\mu_l(k,j)=\big|\mathbf b(\hat\theta_{l,k},\hat r_{l,k})^{\mathsf H}\mathbf b(\hat\theta_{l,j},\hat r_{l,j})\big|,
  \qquad
  \mathrm{score}_{l,k}=\hat\gamma_{l,k}\Big(1-\max_{j\in\mathcal I_k}\hat\mu_l(k,j)^2\Big),
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal I_k$ the strongest co-channel users of $k$. Your simulations create collinear user pairs, where this should help most. With sparse APs, $\hat\mu$ also reflects grating-lobe ambiguities; note that an aliased angle estimate leaves the channel vector correct but makes $\hat\mu$ wrong \cite{chen2024nearfield}, so use the channel vectors directly when angles may alias.

\subsection{P4: Graph-based search with the new objective}
Use the GBSE/GBSA framework \cite{tesolin2026gbse,tesolin2026gbsa} as the optimizer, with an objective that includes the detector (proposed here):
\begin{equation}
  J(\{\mathcal S_k\})=\sum_k \mathrm{SE}_k-\lambda\,K\bar B\quad\text{or}\quad\mathrm{EE}\ \text{(M8) with the gate term},
\end{equation}
started from the P1/P2 solution. GBSE searches Hamming neighbourhoods of the serving-state graph \cite[Sec.~III]{tesolin2026gbse}, and GBSA reaches near-optimal RANEE with linear per-iteration complexity \cite{tesolin2026gbsa}; the warm start should reduce the number of iterations.

\subsection{Recommendation}
\begin{keybox}{What to propose}
\textbf{P1 + P2} as the proposed method (analytical, $\mathcal O(L\log L)$ per user, with Proposition~1 and the gate model), \textbf{P3} as the near-field enhancement, and \textbf{P4} as the near-optimal benchmark. A GNN version \cite{ranasinghe2021gnn,pereira2025gnn} can be future work.
\end{keybox}

\begin{keybox}{Algorithm 1 --- P1+P2: detector-aware clustering (per coherence block, at the CPU)}
\textbf{Input:} estimates $\hat{\mathbf h}_{l,k}$, error covariances $\mathbf C_{l,k}$, power $p$, target $\varepsilon$, gate radius $d_{\rm abs}$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item For every AP $l$: compute the local MMSE combiners $\hat{\mathbf v}_{l,k}$ and the SINRs $\hat\gamma_{l,k}$ from \eqref{eq:p1}.
  \item For every user $k$:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item sort the APs so that $\hat\gamma_{l_1,k}\ge\hat\gamma_{l_2,k}\ge\dots$, and form $\Gamma^{(m)}=\sum_{i\le m}\hat\gamma_{l_i,k}$;
    \item P1: $m_\varepsilon=\min\{m:\Gamma^{(m)}\ge(1-\varepsilon)\Gamma^{(L)}\}$;
    \item P2: $m^\star$ from \eqref{eq:p2};
    \item $\mathcal S_k=\{l_1,\dots,l_{\max(m_\varepsilon,m^\star)}\}$ (use $\min$ for a fronthaul-first design).
  \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Cost:} $\mathcal O(L\log L)$ per user after the combiners, which the receiver computes anyway.
\end{keybox}

\section{How to show the gain (evaluation design)}
\label{sec:eval}

\paragraph{Baselines.} Single-AP; CN/DCC \cite{bjornson2020scalable}; BSR with its own threshold rule \cite[eqs.~(11)--(13)]{mashdour2024study}; effective-gain AP selection \cite{dao2020effective}; GBSE/GBSA with RANEE \cite{tesolin2026gbse,tesolin2026gbsa}; full cooperation.

\paragraph{Regime.} Clustering only matters when interference matters: use at least one of more users ($K=16$--$32$), fewer antennas per AP ($N=16$--$32$), collinear pairs, sparse APs, or estimated CSI. GBSA, for example, uses a small network (6 APs, up to 5 users) to compare with the exhaustive optimum \cite[Sec.~VI]{tesolin2026gbsa}; HybridUA varies the number of users, CPUs and the area \cite[Sec.~IV]{lai2024hybridua}.

\section{Implementation notes for your MATLAB code}
\label{sec:impl}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Compute clusters after channel estimation in each SNR loop, from $\hat{\mathbf h}$ and $\mathbf C$. With \texttt{genie\_csi = false} the estimates are authentic; never use \texttt{h\_det\_all} in a clustering rule for an estimated-CSI result.
  \item Do not tie the cluster size to \texttt{D\_CN}; implement the average-rate threshold of \cite[eqs.~(12)--(13)]{mashdour2024study} for the IR baseline.
  \item Fix the master-AP scale (Issue~1).
  \item Reuse \texttt{etaXap\_acc} ($\rho$), \texttt{loadBSR\_acc} (size) and \texttt{mBERrt*} (per-user BER); add $\kappa_k$, $\Delta_k$ and Jain's index.
  \item Run all rules on the same channel and noise realizations.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{warnbox}{Honest risks}
\begin{itemize}
  \item In the default regime all rules may give the same BER; show the gain as fronthaul and EE at equal BER.
  \item The gate model in P2 assumes a Gaussian fused error and ignores the disks around other points; validate it against the measured rate.
  \item P3 depends on the accuracy of estimated angles and ranges.
  \item The BSR analysis in \cite{mashdour2024study} uses a downlink model; the published letter \cite{mashdour2024wcl} was not available here and may differ in details.
\end{itemize}
\end{warnbox}
